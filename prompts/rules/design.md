DESIGN
- One clear main action per screen; secondary actions look secondary. Most important first and largest; group what belongs together.
- Align to one grid: the header, the content sections and the footer share the same left and right edges (one page width and side padding); inside a section, headings, text, fields and tables start on the same line. Space with a fixed scale, not one-off margins. Everything fits its cell, panel or card (no fixed width or nowrap wider than it).
- Be consistent: the same thing looks the same and sits in the same place on every page. Controls side by side share one height, and one control has one size everywhere: the kit's sizes, never a height or padding of its own. Wording is short and specific; a button says what it does ("Save changes", not "OK"). Headings, table headers, labels and buttons are in sentence case ("Totals", not "TOTALS"); no text-transform: uppercase.
- Every action gets feedback: progress for anything over a second, a quiet confirmation when it worked, errors in plain words where they happened, with the fix. Design the empty, loading and error state of every list, table and chart.
- A dialog only for a decision that blocks the work; details and edits go in place or in a side panel.
- Forms: labels above the fields, one column, sensible defaults, check a field when it is left, never clear what the user typed.
- How data is shown (tables, lists, any kind of chart) is your choice, unless the person names a form. Either way: numbers in a table right-aligned and every table sortable by each of its columns (a header click sorts, a second reverses), and every chart with a title that says what to see. A bar shown with a percentage fills exactly that share of its track (45% is 45% full), not scaled to the largest row.
- Light and dark both work. Never show meaning by colour alone; add a word, icon or pattern.
- Motion is short (100 to 300 ms), eases out, explains a change, and stops when reduced motion is asked for.
- Accessible by default: everything works with the keyboard and shows where the focus is, every image and icon-only button has a name, and text meets WCAG AA contrast.
